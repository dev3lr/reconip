Methodology

Each lookup correlates up to nine independent threat-intelligence, routing and network sources and explains every signal behind the verdict.

Two independent verdicts

Network type (VPN, Tor, datacenter, residential or mobile) and threat history are scored separately, so a clean VPN and a compromised home computer are never confused.

Infrastructure history

Internet routing records show who has announced the address block and since when. The current owner is the network announcing the most specific route. Freshly announced or frequently re-leased blocks raise the threat level.

Neighborhood scan

All 256 addresses in the surrounding /24 are checked against Tor, Spamhaus and proxy/hosting data, revealing risky blocks and attackers who rotate between nearby addresses.

Analyst assessment

A written summary and recommended action generated from the same evidence, by rules or, where enabled, by an AI model.

Results are cached for up to an hour.

Data sources

  • ip-api.com: location, ISP, ASN, proxy / hosting / mobile flags
  • Shodan InternetDB: open ports, service tags, known vulnerabilities
  • GreyNoise: internet-wide scanning and attack observations
  • AbuseIPDB (where enabled): community abuse reports
  • Spamhaus, SpamCop, DroneBL: spam, botnet and exploit blocklists
  • Tor Project: official list of Tor exit nodes
  • RIPEstat: BGP routing history and registry records

Disclaimer

Results are automated assessments based on third-party data and may be incomplete, outdated or wrong. A "clean" result does not prove an IP is safe, and a flagged result does not prove that any person did anything wrong.

IP addresses are often shared (VPNs, mobile carriers, offices) and reassigned over time. Some services, such as residential proxies, are designed to look like home connections.
